Rolling Research: the cadence
that keeps your AI honest.

Jul 29, 2026 5 min read Authoritative

We ran this system for nine months at Thumbtack: 21 studies, 15 product teams served, 94% stakeholder CSAT, 75% more research volume than the prior year's agency pilot. No new headcount. Here's the machine.

The operating loop.

1. Lightweight intake. Teams submit research questions through a simple form. A clear question and a timeline — that's the whole brief. Removing kickoff overhead is intentional: the harder it is to request research, the fewer requests arrive, and the important-but-unasked questions accumulate invisibly. When asking is easy, people ask.

2. Weekly triage. Every Friday, review and prioritize the queue. What needs a full moderated study? What can be answered with a targeted survey? What's urgent enough to kick off Monday? Triage keeps the practice honest — it prevents the loudest stakeholder from automatically jumping the line, and it catches questions that sound like research needs but are actually policy decisions in disguise.

3. Sessions throughout the week. Interviews, usability tests, concept evaluations, surveys — whatever the question requires. Recruiting happens in parallel with protocol development. Most projects complete within two weeks of intake.

4. Flash Findings. The output is always the same: an executive summary and direct answers to the questions asked. Short enough that people read it. Structured consistently so insights can be compared across studies. No 60-slide decks. No researcher-voice editorializing. Just what the team needs to move.

5. Embedded presence. Insights land in Slack as sessions run — not in a final readout three weeks later. Stakeholders see real users regularly, not quarterly. This changes the organizational relationship to research: it stops being a service you request and becomes an environment you live in.

Where this connects to the AI thesis.

That last point is where this series has been heading. A Flash Finding isn't just for the humans. A consistent, dated, structured output format is exactly what your company's AI and RAG systems need to stay synced with reality. When findings are consistently formatted and date-stamped, your retrieval system knows how current they are. When they answer specific questions in a predictable structure, they're retrievable against specific queries. Weekly cadence in, accurate assistant out.

Cadence is the technology. The rest is logistics.

Why this is rare.

None of this is radical. Teresa Torres has argued for years that strong product teams maintain weekly customer touchpoints — continuous discovery is the stated operating model for high-performing product organizations. What's rare isn't the idea. It's the operational machinery that actually survives contact with everyone's calendar.

Most research cadences fail not because the concept is wrong but because the process is too heavy to sustain. Kickoffs that require two weeks to schedule. Briefs that take a week to write. Synthesis that blocks on the researcher's availability. Every friction point is a study that doesn't happen, a question that stays unanswered, a week of signal that doesn't flow into the system.

The intake form, the Friday triage, the Flash Findings format — these are specifically engineered to remove those friction points. The goal is a cadence so lightweight that it can keep running even when things get busy, because busy is the permanent state of every product team.
